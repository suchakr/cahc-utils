Narration

Summary

  • Computed for latitude 30°N with symmetric heliacal criteria: first morning rise and last evening setting with the Sun at -10° altitude.
  • In the -1500 series, the northernmost rising azimuth is Svātī, while the southernmost is Mūla.
  • In the -1500 setting series, the westernmost setting azimuth is Mūla, while the easternmost is Svātī.
  • Largest azimuth shifts from -1500 to -500 are Svātī +7.10°, Bharaṇī +6.54°, Viśākhā +6.48°.
  • Largest setting-azimuth shifts from -1500 to -500 are Svātī +7.10°, Bharaṇī +6.54°, Viśākhā +6.48°.
  • Largest declination shifts from -1500 to -500 are Bharaṇī +5.62°, Svātī +5.62°, Viśākhā +5.61°.
  • The rise/set expansion is geometrically constrained: for a fixed latitude and flat horizon, setting azimuth is largely the complementary horizon expression of the same declination signal, and rise/set declination will often look nearly identical.
  • The table includes UTC ISO timestamps and Julian Dates so the event moments can be reused directly in Stellarium or related tools.
